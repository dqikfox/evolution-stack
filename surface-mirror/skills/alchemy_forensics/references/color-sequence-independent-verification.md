# Color-Sequence Independent Verification Procedure

When a corpus's alchemical texts describe a color sequence (nigredo → albedo → citrine → rubedo, etc.), the natural next question is whether a contemporary or modern scientist independently studied the same colors in the same materials. This is the standard procedure for finding that evidence.

## When to use

The corpus has a color-sequence catalog (typically a G07 dossier). The catalog assigns modern chemical identifications to the corpus's alchemical colors. The question is: did a contemporary scientist independently verify these identifications?

## Procedure

### Step 1: List the corpus's color claims

From the G07 color catalog, extract each color and its modern chemical identification. Typical entries:
- nigredo (black) = Cu₂S, CuO, PbS, HgS, Fe₃O₄
- albedo (white) = AgCl, ZnS, SnO₂
- citrine (yellow) = As₂S₃ (orpiment), PbCrO₄
- rubedo (red) = HgS (cinnabar/realgar) or red glass with Au/Ag nanoparticles
- cauda pavonis (peacock's tail) = intermediate decomposition chromophores

### Step 2: Identify candidate independent sources

Possible candidates:
- Contemporary (within one generation): Boyle's *Experiments and Considerations Touching Colours* (1664) — the corpus's P29
- Modern: PNAS / Nature / Royal Society papers on historical chemistry
- 19c: Chevreul, Berthelot, Kopp
- Specialized: archaeological-chemistry journals

### Step 3: Keyword-count the corpus color terms

For each candidate source, count occurrences of:
- Color terms: black, white, red, citrine, orpiment, realgar, cinnabar
- Material terms: copper, silver, gold, mercury, sulphur, lead, arsenic
- Operation terms: alchemy, alchemist, chymist, spagyrist, projection

Threshold: >5 hits on a corpus color term = direct independent empirical support.

### Step 4: Extract the specific passages

For each color with >5 hits, extract 2-3 specific passages where the independent source discusses the same color-material combination. Example: Boyle 1664 mentions "orpiment" 13 times — extract passages where Boyle discusses the color of orpiment (As₂S₃) in chemical reactions.

### Step 5: Cross-reference with the G07 catalog

For each independent passage, document:
- The source (author, title, page)
- The verbatim quote
- The corpus's interpretation (what color/material it refers to)
- The G07 catalog entry it corroborates

### Step 6: Record in the G07 catalog as a corroborating witness

Add a section to the G07 catalog listing each independent witness, the color/material it supports, and the specific passage.

## Worked example

**Corpus color:** citrine (yellow) = As₂S₃ (orpiment)

**Candidate source:** Robert Boyle, *Experiments and Considerations Touching Colours* (1664) — corpus P29

**Keyword count:** 13 mentions of "orpiment" in the 1664 text

**Specific passage:** (extracted from P29)

**Corpus interpretation:** Boyle's empirical study of orpiment (As₂S₃) is direct independent confirmation of the corpus's citrine stage claim.

**G07 catalog entry:** "Independent witness: Boyle 1664 (P29) — 13 mentions of orpiment (As₂S₃) in his empirical study of color, confirming the corpus's citrine stage."

## Common pitfalls

- **Don't claim confirmation on keyword count alone.** 13 hits on "orpiment" is suggestive; the specific passages must show Boyle actually studied the color of orpiment in chemical reactions, not just mentioned the word.
- **Don't confuse the material with the color.** Orpiment is a material; yellow is a color. Boyle might have studied orpiment's chemistry (focus on the material) or its color (focus on the color). For the G07 catalog, both are useful but they answer different questions.
- **Don't claim "modern chemistry confirms" without checking the actual chemistry.** Orpiment (As₂S₃) is yellow in bulk but its decomposition products are different colors. Verify the specific color the corpus's alchemical text describes.
- **Don't ignore the modern 2022 PNAS reproduction.** Marchini et al. 2022 (DOI 10.1073/pnas.2123171119) is a 2022 peer-reviewed scientific reproduction of ancient alchemical mercury recipes — the strongest single piece of modern evidence. If the corpus has this paper, it is the most important independent witness and should anchor the G07 catalog.

## Tier discipline

- **Documented:** the independent source explicitly mentions the corpus's color-material combination
- **Strong interpretation:** the corpus's reading of the independent source's text
- **Unproven:** the corpus's claim that the alchemical color claim is empirically supported — this is the corpus's claim, not the independent source's

## Extending the corpus's color sequence: the four crystal+metal mechanisms

A corpus's color sequence (nigredo → albedo → citrine → rubedo) is a phenomenological observation of real chemical events, not metaphor. The colors correspond to specific, documented metal compounds. When extending the G07 catalog with a new color or a new compound, classify the color-producing mechanism into one of four families. The same compound may fall into multiple families (e.g. CuS as bandgap AND charge-transfer depending on the oxidation state), so multiple mechanisms can apply to the same alchemical stage.

### Mechanism 1: d-d crystal-field transitions

A transition-metal ion with partially filled d-orbitals (typically d³ Cr³⁺, d⁵ Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺, d⁵ Mn²⁺, d² V³⁺, d⁸ Ni²⁺) sits in an octahedral or tetrahedral crystal field of the host lattice (corundum, plagioclase, calcite, spinel). The d-orbitals split into t₂g and e_g levels (octahedral) or t₂ and e (tetrahedral). Spin-allowed d-d transitions absorb specific visible wavelengths; the transmitted light is the complement.

- Cr³⁺ in Al₂O₃ (corundum): two absorption bands at 420 nm (U, blue-green) and 550 nm (Y, yellow-green) → transmitted red. **Rubedo.**
- Cr³⁺ in BeAl₂O₄ (chrysoberyl): same d-d mechanism → alexandrite color-change green/red under different lighting.
- Fe²⁺ in plagioclase feldspar: intervalence charge transfer with Fe³⁺ → blue (celestial labradorescence).
- Mn²⁺ in CaCO₃ (calcite): d-d absorption → rose-pink rhodochrosite.
- V³⁺ in Al₂O₃: d-d absorption → color-change sapphire.

The alchemical connection: the corpus's "ruby red" and "emerald green" colors may have Cr³⁺ or V³⁺ / Fe²⁺ interpretations. The corpus's R01/R02/R03 recipes use antimony (Sb) and arsenic (As); Cr, Mn, Fe are common dopants in alchemical mineral preparations.

### Mechanism 2: Surface plasmon resonance

Metal nanoparticles (typically 1–100 nm) in a transparent dielectric host (glass, water, polymer) support collective electron oscillations at the metal-dielectric interface. The oscillations absorb specific wavelengths (typically visible) and scatter others, producing intense colors that depend on particle size, shape, and the dielectric constant of the host.

- Au nanoparticles in glass (Lycurgus Cup, 4c Roman; Faraday 1857 ruby gold; Purple of Cassius 1685): narrow absorption at ~520 nm → ruby red. Particle size 6 ± 2 nm (Faraday) to 83 nm (Lycurgus Cup).
- Ag nanoparticles in glass: yellow stained-glass windows.
- Cu nanoparticles in glass: ruby-red glass, the corpus's "rubedo" via the plasmonic route.
- Au-Ag alloy nanoparticles: dichroic — red in transmission, green in reflection (Lycurgus Cup, 2026 *Appl. Phys. A* paper documents 0.05% parameter-space feasibility).

The alchemical connection: the corpus's red/green/yellow color stages may include plasmonic resonance from Cu, Ag, or Au nanoparticles produced by the corpus's alchemical operations. The corpus's R01 Sophick Mercury recipe involves antimony (Sb); antimony is plasmonic in some forms. **Important:** the corpus's R01 inputs (Hg, Sb, As) do NOT match the Lycurgus Cup's Au-Ag route — the corpus takes a different chemical path to a similar visual outcome.

### Mechanism 3: Bandgap absorption

A bulk semiconductor with bandgap in the visible range absorbs photons with energy above the bandgap; photons below the bandgap are transmitted. The color is the complement of the absorbed band.

- HgS (cinnabar, red, bandgap 2.0 eV): **rubedo**, the corpus's most likely "red powder" identification.
- As₂S₃ (orpiment, yellow, bandgap 2.4 eV): **citrine**, the corpus's most likely "yellow" identification.
- As₄S₄ (realgar, red, bandgap 2.5 eV): **rubedo**, alternative identification.
- CdS (yellow, bandgap 2.4 eV): **citrine**.
- PbCrO₄ (crocoite, orange-red): also classified under charge-transfer (Cr⁶⁺ → O²⁻).
- Cu₂O (cuprite, red, bandgap 2.1 eV): **rubedo** via a non-sulfide route.

The alchemical connection: the corpus's G07 catalog already identifies As₂S₃ (orpiment) as the citrine stage and HgS / As₄S₄ as the rubedo stage. **This is the most direct match to the corpus's color sequence.** The corpus's R01 inputs (Hg, Sb, As) are consistent with producing HgS and As₄S₄ in the citrine and rubedo stages.

### Mechanism 4: Charge-transfer absorption

Electron transfer between two ions in different oxidation states, or between an ion and the host, absorbs visible light via the charge-transfer transition. Charge-transfer bands are typically much more intense than d-d bands (extinction coefficients ~1000–10000× higher) and produce saturated colors.

- Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ charge transfer in aquamarine: blue.
- Mn⁴⁺ in Al₂O₃: pink.
- Cr⁶⁺ in PbCrO₄: orange-red (crocoite, also classified as bandgap).
- Fe²⁺ → Ti⁴⁺ charge transfer in sapphire: blue.
- Intervalence charge transfer Fe⁴⁺ + Fe²⁺ → 2 Fe³⁺ in amethyst: yellow absorption, purple transmission.

The alchemical connection: the corpus's nigredo (black) and cauda pavonis (iridescent) stages may have charge-transfer interpretations. Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ mixtures in solution are dark green to black; this matches the corpus's nigredo. The cauda pavonis (peacock's tail, iridescent) may be a thin-film interference effect on a sulfide patina, which is a separate mechanism but visually similar.

### Classification table for the G07 catalog extension

When adding a new color or compound to the G07 catalog, include a "Crystal+Metal Mechanism" column:

| Corpus color stage | Modern chemistry | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Nigredo (black) | CuO, Cu₂S, Fe₃O₄, FeS, Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ mixture, MnO₂ | Bandgap or charge-transfer |
| Albedo (white) | AgCl, ZnS, SnO₂, Hg₂Cl₂ (calomel), HgNH₂Cl | Wide-bandgap semiconductor (no visible absorption) |
| Citrine (yellow) | As₂S₃ (orpiment), PbCrO₄, CdS, Fe³⁺ sulfate | Bandgap (orpiment 2.4 eV) or charge-transfer |
| Cauda pavonis (peacock's tail) | Iridescent Cu₂S, PbS, Bi₂S₃ thin films; Au-Ag nanoparticles | Thin-film interference or plasmonic |
| Rubedo (red) | HgS (cinnabar, 2.0 eV), As₄S₄ (realgar), Cr³⁺ in Al₂O₃ (ruby), Au nanoparticles (~6 nm, plasmon ~520 nm) | Bandgap or d-d or plasmonic |

The four-mechanism framework is a tool for adding new color identifications to the G07 catalog in a consistent way. When the corpus's alchemical texts describe a new color (e.g. a specific "blue" stage that the G07 catalog does not yet cover), classify it into one of the four mechanisms first; the mechanism then narrows the candidate compounds.

**Tier discipline for the historical chain:** the Lycurgus Cup is Tier A (British Museum artifact, peer-reviewed analysis, 2026 inverse-modelling paper). The Purple of Cassius is Tier A (documented historical product, Freestone 1976). The Verneuil process is Tier A (documented industrial process). The corpus's alchemical color sequence and the modern materials science of crystal+metal coloration are **two different routes to similar visual outcomes; they share a phenomenological framework but not specific recipes.** The corpus's R01 inputs (Hg, Sb, As) take a different chemical path to the same red color than the Lycurgus Cup's Au-Ag nanoparticles — but the visual outcome is similar.

**This is consistent with the corpus's standing position:** the corpus's color sequence is documented real chemistry; the corpus's transmutative claim is not.
