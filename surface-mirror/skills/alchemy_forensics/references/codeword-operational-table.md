# Corpus codewords → operations

Standing reference: codeword-to-chemical-operation map derived from
cross-corpus source-criticism on 17c English alchemy (Philalethes / Starkey
corpus, Mirror of Alchemy, Marrow of Alchemy, Ripley Reviv'd). When a
codeword is used in a corpus text, the operational definition comes from
the contemporary prose source (Sci-Hist MS 13 for Starkey, Newton's
handwritten Index Chemicus for 17c chemistry).

## Established operational definitions (tier: documented)

### Eagle

A **single cohobation cycle** — one redistillation of the sulfur-charged
acidic menstruum in a sealed vessel. The volatile component rises (the
"Eagle flies"), condenses on the upper part of the vessel, and returns
to the body of the menstruum. Visual event: vapor rising and returning.
Time per cycle: **half a day** (Starkey via Newton Sci-Hist MS 13: *"in
diem dimidium Aquilam unam praeparare possum"* = "in half a day I can
prepare one Eagle"). Modern reproduction: 500 mL RBF, Allihn condenser,
heating mantle, 200 mL conc. H₂SO₄ + 10 g flowers S at 200 °C, cool to
60 °C, add 5 mL 30% H₂O₂ dropwise, Eagle 1 at 180 °C closed loop 1 hour,
add 1 g S, Eagle 2, etc., through Eagle 7. Test: copper strip dipped in
5-10 sec → lustrous black Cu₂S patina (exact Theion Hydor result Leiden
X prescribes). Standard Eagle count: **7 or 9 "most desiredly"** (P17
printed 258 / PDF 332 canonical passage); seven Eagles = seven
cohobations (printed 58 / PDF 132 Philalethes preface).

### Doves of Diana

Two meanings in the corpus, both pointing at the same material identity:

1. **Visible product of the operation** (Marrow stanzas 16-20): a
   silver-white precipitate that forms when a metal is dissolved by the
   Green Lyon (a corrosive acidic menstruum). The Doves appear "in
   shining bright attire" with "pure silver wing" — visual description
   consistent with silver chloride (AgCl), a white curdy precipitate.
2. **Alternative route** (Marrow stanza 66): *"Some use Dianaes Doves
   for to prepare / the water, which a tedious labour is"* — a
   silver-precipitation procedure (likely AgCl from silver + aqua fortis
   + salt) used as a starting material for the menstruum.

The Doves route is **deprecated** in favor of the R01 salt-vinegar
distillation route (Sophick Mercury). Yield: ~33% (2:1 failure rate
per Marrow stanza 66). Both routes aim at the same material identity
(the Sophick Mercury / menstruum).

### Green Lyon

A **corrosive acidic menstruum** — the prepared reagent that dissolves
base metal. Marrow stanzas 16, 18, 20 name it as the operator's
reagent, used to subdue a beast (a base metal). Likely identity: the
Sophick Mercury itself (R01) or an arsenic-bearing analogue. The Green
Lyon produces the Doves of Diana as a visible product (silver-white
precipitate from the dissolved metal).

### Dragon

A **metal being dissolved** — the base metal that is the target of the
operation. "Infolded Dragon" = metal in solution, having lost its
metallic form. The Dragon's "sting" = the metal's resistance to
corrosion; the Doves' presence causes the sting to be lost.

### Vulcan

**Fire** — the active agent of transformation. Marrow stanzas 16, 20
name Vulcan as the agent that revives the dissolved Dragon. In modern
chemistry: furnace heat, controlled by the fire regime (gentle then
increasing, per Mirror Chapter IV).

### Cadmus

Named in Marrow stanza 16 as a "sociate" (companion) of the Green
Lyon. **Probable identity: an alloy or mineral that helps the Lyon
dissolve metal** — possibly antimony or a copper-bearing alloy, given
the standard 17c mineral-acid use of antimony as a "philosophical
assistant" in metallurgical operations. Verify via Marrow
stanza-context.

### Diana

**Silver** — the celestial symbol, standard 17c alchemical code. "Dianas
Doves" = silver-bearing precipitate. "Diana with a little copper" =
silver tinged with copper (a greenish-celadon product of the R01
operation).

### Venus

**Copper** — the celestial symbol, standard 17c alchemical code.
Stanza 65 of the Marrow names "Venus mediation" as the operation that
allows the souls of Saturn (lead) and Mars (iron) to be separated by
Diana (silver). Modern interpretation: copper's affinity for both lead
and iron makes it a "mediator" in metallurgical operations; or copper's
role in the production of vitriol (CuSO₄, "oil of Venus") and its use
in mineral preparations makes it a "mediating" reagent.

### Saturn / Mars / Sol / Luna

Lead / Iron / Gold / Silver — the standard 17c celestial-planetary
correspondences. Always identify as the metal, not the planet, in
modern reconstructions.

### Chalybs / Acier / Steel (magnet-steel metaphor)

**Not** the literal metal steel. In 17c alchemical usage, *Chalybs*
(Latin), *Acier* (French), and "steel" (English) name the **attractive
property of the Universal Spirit** (Sal Caeleste / Esprit Universel /
Sophick Mercury). The metaphor: as the magnet (the Universal Spirit)
attracts steel (the metallic substrate), so the seed of Gold attracts
Mercury. Three primary-source confirmations:

- Helvetius *Vitulus Aureus* 1667 (P30 p71): "**SENDIVOGII Chalybs sit
  vera Mercurialis humiditas Metallica**" = "Sendivogius's Chalybs is the
  true Mercurial metallic humidity" — a specific alchemical concept, not
  raw steel.
- Cosmopolite (Sendivogius) *Novum Lumen Chymicum* 1691 p394
  (corpus's *Cosmopolite* PDF, French 2011 Geneva ed.): "tantôt Acier,
  parce que comme l'Aimant attire l'Acier, ainsi la semence de l'Or
  attire ledit Mercure" = "sometimes Steel, because as the Magnet
  attracts Steel, so the seed of Gold attracts said Mercury."
- Newton Chymistry Project (M25 corpus): Newton's "aerial niter" maps
  to the same concept.

When a 17c alchemical text uses *Chalybs* / *Acier* / "steel" / magnet /
attraction language, decode the passage as: the Universal Spirit's
attractive property acting on a metallic substrate. **Do not** interpret
"steel" as the modern alloy Fe-C. To confirm, look for the *other*
author's primary text the corpus is citing (Helvetius → Sendivogius's
*Novum Lumen Chymicum*; Newton → Index Chemicus); the cited text
usually makes the metaphor explicit.

### Universal Spirit / Sal Caeleste / Sophick Mercury / aerial niter

These are **the same concept under different names** across the 17c
alchemical and proto-chemical tradition:

| Name | Language | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Esprit Universel | French | Cosmopolite/Sendivogius *Novum Lumen* 1691 |
| Sal Caeleste / Sal quoddam caeleste | Latin | Helvetius *Vitulus Aureus* 1667 (P30) |
| Sophick Mercury | English | Philalethes/Starkey 1654+ (R01) |
| Aerial niter | English/Latin | Newton Chymistry Project (M25) |
| Caeleste / "celestial salt" | English | Helvetius 1670 English translation |

The concept: a celestial spirit (air-borne, salt-bearing) that pervades
all matter, is the source of life/growth, and is the active agent of
metallic generation. When a corpus text names any of these, look for the
**magnet/attraction metaphor** and the **three Principles (Mercury,
Sulphur, Salt)** framework — both are diagnostic of the same standing
concept. **Do not** treat "Sophick Mercury" and "Sal Caeleste" as two
different materials; the corpus's 17c alchemical authors are using
different names for one alchemical concept.

## Color sequence (consensus across the corpus)

The nigredo-albedo-citrinitas-rubedo sequence appears in:

- Leiden Papyrus X (3c Greek-Egyptian, earliest attestation)
- Speculum Alchemiae / Mirror of Alchemy (13-14c Latin / 1597 English)
- Marrow of Alchemy (1655 English verse)
- Three Tracts 1668 (Amsterdam Philalethes)
- Ripley Reviv'd 1678 (English)

Sequence: **black (nigredo) → red transient (citrinitas) → green (the
"green Lyon" stage) → peacock's tail (cauda pavonis) → white (albedo) →
ash (transition) → red (rubedo)**. The sequence is **consensus across
the corpus**, not a per-text idiosyncrasy. When a text specifies a
color sequence, expect the standard sequence unless the text
explicitly says otherwise.

## Apparatus language (consensus across the corpus)

| Corpus term | Modern interpretation |
|---|---|
| "round glass vessel with small neck" | Florence flask / RBF |
| "sealed with lute" | Luted joint, ground-glass or wax-sealed |
| "closed outer vessel" | Double-walled or sand-bath containment |
| "furnace that imitates the mineral mountain" | Closed furnace, retaining heat |
| "iron alembic with iron cucurbit" | Iron distillation head + iron body (used in R01 for mercury) |
| "secret vessel, Garden of the Sages" | Standard 17c sealed distillation apparatus |
| "artificial fire" | Furnace heat, controlled by the operator |
| "natural fire which increases" | Gentle then increasing heat regime |

## Fire regime (consensus)

- **Gentle fire first**, then **increasing "by little and little"** —
  Aristotelian biological metaphor: light meats first (infant), then
  stronger (mature). Mirror Chapter IV: *"this work is very like to the
  creation of man: for as the Infant in the beginning is nourished with
  light meats, but the bones being strengthened with stronger: so this
  mastery also, first it must have an easy fire."*
- **R01 alternative**: warm bath → sixth degree (Sci-Hist MS 13).
- **Standard 17c fire degrees** (Boyle, Kunckel, etc.): first degree
  = body temperature; second = milk-warm; third = bath; fourth = boiling
  water; fifth = boiling oil; sixth = sealing heat (≈ 250-300 °C);
  seventh = glowing coal (≈ 500-600 °C).
