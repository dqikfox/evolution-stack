# Plain-English Dossier Template

When the user asks for a "plain English" or "anyone can understand" version of a technical dossier, use this template. The plain-English dossier is *not a summary* — it is the same content rendered for a non-expert audience, with every term explained and every tier-discipline claim labeled.

## Section structure

### Section 1 — What's actually in the corpus?
A table of major files with: file ID, what it is, when, language. No more than 12 rows. Each row's "what it is" is a single sentence a non-expert can read.

### Section 2 — What is a fact? What is interpretation? What is unproven?
The tier discipline explained in plain English. **Each tier must be defined in a single sentence at the top:**
- **FACT (Documented):** things that can be verified from the primary sources themselves
- **STRONG INTERPRETATION (well-supported but not certain):** claims that follow from the evidence but are not directly stated
- **UNPROVEN (cannot be established from the corpus alone):** claims the corpus makes but cannot independently verify

Then for each major claim, state which tier it falls into. Use a bulleted list, not a table (tables are for experts).

### Section 3 — The single most important evidence
Identify the one piece of evidence the non-expert reader must understand. Explain it in 2-3 paragraphs. State the source, what was done, what was found, and what it means for the corpus.

### Section 4 — The chain of evidence
A chronological list of the corpus's main sources. For each: year, author, contribution, one-line significance. ~10-12 entries.

### Section 4B (optional) — Witnesses and counter-witnesses
When the corpus has contemporary scientists who support OR oppose the alchemical claims, list them with one-line characterizations. This is the "three-way dialogue" from the technical dossier.

### Section 5 — What does "[technical term]" mean, in plain English?
For every technical term used in the dossier, add a one-paragraph plain-English explanation. Common terms that need this:
- Transmutation
- Alkahest
- Sophick Mercury
- Philosophical Arsenic
- The Elixir
- The Stone / Philosophers Stone
- Sulphur / Mercury / Salt (the principles, not the elements)
- The Eagles
- The Doves
- Adeptist
- Spagyrist / Chymist
- Magnum Opus / Great Work

### Section 6 — What was real, and what was symbolic?
A guide for the non-expert reader to distinguish the corpus's real chemistry from its allegorical language. Three categories:
- **Real chemistry (reproducible in modern labs)** — with a modern chemistry name for each
- **Symbolic/allegorical (real chemistry hidden under imagery)** — with the underlying chemistry
- **Unclear (could be real, could be symbolic)** — with the corpus's own ambiguity

### Section 7 — What's the bottom line?
Three sections:
- What the corpus establishes
- What the corpus does NOT establish
- The standing position (in one sentence)

### Section 8 — Recommendations
For the user and the corpus. Concrete next steps.

## Writing rules

- **No assumed knowledge.** A reader with no alchemical background must be able to follow.
- **No Latin without translation.** When a Latin term is used, translate it in parentheses.
- **No jargon without explanation.** "Sophick Mercury" → "(Starkey's name for the prepared reagent)".
- **No direct PDF page references without context.** "(P17 PDF p471)" → "(in the Ripley Reviv'd 1678 text, on page 471 of the PDF)".
- **Tier discipline maintained.** Every claim labeled Documented / Strong interpretation / Unproven.
- **Same depth as the technical dossier.** The plain-English dossier is auditable on its own.

## Worked example

**Technical dossier:** "The corpus's R01 Sophick Mercury recipe from Sci-Hist MS 13 specifies: Inveni meliorem purgandi viam per acetum et purum salem marinum, sic intra diem dimidium Aquilam unam praeparare possum (P17 printed 258). One Eagle = one cohobation cycle of the mercury-arsenic compound. Standard = 7-9 Eagles, 9 months."

**Plain-English version:** "Starkey (writing as Philalethes) says in his handbook: 'I have found a better way of purging, through vinegar and pure sea salt, so that in half a day I can prepare one Eagle' (in the Ripley Reviv'd 1678 text). An 'Eagle' is one round of distillation and purification of the liquid mixture. The standard recipe calls for 7 to 9 Eagles, spread over about 9 months."

## Common pitfalls

- **Don't summarize.** A 1-page summary of a 30-page dossier is not a plain-English version — it's a summary. The plain-English version is the same depth, different audience.
- **Don't drop the tier discipline.** Plain-English ≠ uncritical. Every claim must still be labeled.
- **Don't add new claims.** The plain-English dossier says what the technical dossier says, in different words. If a claim isn't in the technical dossier, it doesn't belong here.
- **Don't soften the unproven claims.** Transmutation is unproven in the technical dossier; it stays unproven in the plain-English version. Plain-English ≠ hopeful.
