# Counter-Evidence and Three-Way Dialogue Schema

When a corpus's alchemical theory has a known contemporary scientific objection, document the objection as a first-class deliverable using the schema below. The counter-evidence dossier is *equal evidentiary weight* with the supporting dossiers.

## When to write a counter-evidence dossier

- A contemporary scientist (within one generation of the corpus's main texts) explicitly attacks the corpus's theoretical framework.
- A modern scientific assessment (e.g. PNAS, Nature, Royal Society) is in the corpus and reaches a different conclusion.
- The corpus's own alchemical authors disagree with each other on a key point (internal critique, e.g. Petrus Bonus 1320 vs Philalethes 1654+ on arsenic).

## Schema

### Section 1 — Bibliographic data
- Author, title, publisher, year, city
- Corpus location (relative path)
- Length / pages
- Author's institutional position (e.g. Fellow of the Royal Society, "Father of Modern Chemistry")
- Significance (founding document, peer-reviewed paper, etc.)

### Section 2 — Why this is the corpus's counter-evidence
- What corpus theory is being challenged
- The critic's exact words (verbatim quote)
- Translation into plain English

### Section 3 — Key passages
- For each major attack: verbatim quote, page reference, surrounding context
- 3-5 passages is typical

### Section 4 — What the critic is NOT saying
- Distinguish what the critic is attacking from what they are NOT attacking
- This is the most important section — it prevents mischaracterization
- Example: Boyle 1661 attacks Salt-Sulphur-Mercury theory but does NOT deny chymical experiments themselves

### Section 5 — Updated standing position
- What changes in the corpus's standing position in light of the objection
- What stays the same
- What new questions the objection opens

### Section 6 — The three-way dialogue
When the corpus has all three 17c positions represented, document the three-way dialogue:

1. **The alchemists** (Starkey/Philalethes, Helvetius, Ashmole, Valentine) — claim transmutation is real
2. **The modern chemists** (Boyle, Hooke, Royal Society) — reject alchemical theory of matter
3. **The empirical natural philosophers** (Helmont, willow experiment 1648) — bridge position, use alchemical apparatus but do modern-style experiments

The corpus's R01/R02/R03 dossiers are aligned with position (1) but their *chemical products* are aligned with position (3) (Helmont's mass balance approach). Position (2) is the corpus's strongest contemporary critic.

## Tier discipline (apply to every claim in the dossier)

- **Documented:** the critic's text says this verbatim
- **Strong interpretation:** the corpus's reading of the critic's text
- **Unproven:** the corpus's standing position in light of the critic (this is the corpus's claim, not the critic's)

## Worked example — G08C (Boyle 1661 + Boyle 1664 + Petrus Bonus 1320)

**Documented:** Boyle 1661 *Sceptical Chymist* title page reads "Chymico-Physical Doubts & Paradoxes, Touching the Spagyrist's Principles Commonly call'd Hypostatical, As they are wont to be Propos'd and Defended by the Generality of ALCHYMISTS."

**Documented:** Boyle 1661 attacks "the grand Doctrine of the Chymists touching their three Hypostatical Principles" (Salt, Sulphur, Mercury).

**Strong interpretation:** Boyle is attacking the *theory*, not the *practice* — he says "I am a great Lover of Chymical Experiments" and "I have no mean esteem of divers Chymical Remedies."

**Unproven:** The corpus's standing position that transmutation is unproven — this is the corpus's claim, not Boyle's (Boyle does not specifically deny transmutation; he attacks the theory of matter).

## Common pitfalls

- **Don't confuse the critic's position with the corpus's position.** The critic says X; the corpus says Y. The dossier documents both.
- **Don't quote the critic out of context.** Always include the surrounding passage to prevent mischaracterization.
- **Don't make the critic say more than they said.** If Boyle says "I am against the three Hypostatical Principles," don't claim Boyle says "transmutation is impossible" (he doesn't).
- **Don't bury the counter-evidence in a footnote.** A counter-evidence dossier is a first-class deliverable.
