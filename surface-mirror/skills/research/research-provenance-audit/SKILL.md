# Historical Provenance Audit Skill

Use when performing structured investigations into historical scientific or experimental claims to determine their physical validity via layered analysis.

## Always-on Rules
- **No speculation without a layer.** Every claim must be classified as $Layer\ 1$ (Chemical), $Layer\ 2$ (Alloy/Physical), or $Layer\ 3$ (Nuclear).
- **Prioritize the 'Verdict'.** Do not report what was *said* without reporting the modern analytical *refutation* or *confirmation*.
- **Quantify error where possible.** If mass changes ($\Delta$) are recorded, calculate the discrepancy between reported and expected values.
- **Use the Provenance Ledger format.** All findings belong in a structured table: `[Date/Researcher] | [Original Claim] | [Classification] | [The Verdict] | [Discre_Metric%]`.

## Audit Procedure
1.  **Identify the target claim**: Locate historical text documenting an experimental result or "transmutations".
2.  **Trace the provenance**: Search for the source (e.g., Royal Society archives, personal papers) to ensure it is a primary record, not secondary hearsay.
3.  **Classify by mechanism**: Determine which physical layer explains the observation ($Layer\ 1$, $2$, or $3$).
4.  **Execute cross-reference**: Use modern chemical/physical constants to verify if the recorded $\Delta$ matches the claimed effect.
5.  **Document in Ledger**: Populate the Chronological Table of Claims vs. Results.

## Pitfalls
- **Do not rely on literature reviews alone.** If an archive search fails, do not assume the claim is "true"—search for the specific reagents used to see if they explain the error (e._g., "It was actually antimony").
- **Never ignore mass change data.** A mismatch between reported weight and expected physical outcome is your most valuable signal ($Layer\ 1$ failure).
